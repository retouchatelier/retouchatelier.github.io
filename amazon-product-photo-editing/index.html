What we deliver

One shoot, three marketplaces, three different rule sets

Almost nobody sells on Amazon only. The same product gets shot once and then has to satisfy Amazon, a Shopify store and often Etsy — and the three disagree with each other in ways that cost a reshoot if nobody plans for it.

Where it goesWhat it has to be
Amazon main imageThe strictest of the three. Pure white, product filling most of the frame, nothing else in shot — no props, no text, no logo. This is the one with consequences attached.
Your own storeNo rules at all, which is the problem. Square or 4:5, consistent margin, and a background that matches your theme rather than Amazon's white. Shopify image editing.
EtsyRewards styled and in-context images where Amazon forbids them on the main. A frame that works on Etsy will often fail Amazon's main-image rule outright. Etsy photo editing.

The practical consequence: the Amazon main image is a derivative, not the master. Shoot and retouch for the strictest rule set, then cut the looser versions from the same frame. Done the other way round — styling first, stripping later — you end up reshooting, because you cannot remove a prop that was holding the product up.

We deliver all three sets from one batch, in each channel's own naming convention, so your team is not renaming files by hand. And a point worth saying plainly: compliance is the floor, not the goal. Passing Amazon's rules stops a listing being suppressed; it does nothing on its own for whether anyone clicks it.

Marketplace requirements change. Everything we deliver is checked against the current published rules before it leaves the studio — and if you want the specification in writing before you commit, ask and you will have it the same working day.

A listing image that breaks Amazon’s rules costs you the Buy Box, not just a redo. Every file we return is checked against the main-image standard before it leaves the studio.

Pure white main image

Main images are delivered on true RGB 255,255,255 white, not the off-white a camera actually captures. The product keeps its own edge and shadow so it never looks pasted onto the background.

85% frame fill

Each product is scaled and centred so it occupies roughly 85% of the frame, the ratio Amazon expects. Consistent framing across a catalogue also stops your gallery looking uneven on mobile.

Clean edges & shadow

Hand-drawn paths rather than one-click selections, so hair, fabric, cables and transparent packaging keep believable edges. Natural or drop shadows are added where they help the product sit on the page.

Resolution & format

Files are delivered at the longest side Amazon needs for zoom, in sRGB, correctly sharpened for screen, and named to match your SKU sheet so bulk upload is a drag-and-drop.

How long does Amazon image editing take?

Standard turnaround is 24 to 48 hours. Large catalogue uploads are scheduled in batches at an agreed daily volume, and rush delivery is available when a launch date is fixed.

Will the images meet Amazon’s main image requirements?

Yes. Main images are delivered on pure white, with the product filling roughly 85% of the frame, at zoom-ready resolution in sRGB. If Amazon ever flags an image we edited, we correct it at no charge.

Can you handle hundreds of SKUs?

Yes. We run bulk catalogues with a dedicated team and an agreed daily throughput, with a second retoucher checking the set for consistent colour, scale and shadow before delivery.

Do you rename files to match my SKUs?

Yes. Send your SKU sheet or naming convention and files come back named and foldered to match, so bulk upload needs no manual sorting.

Do you use generative AI?

No. We correct and refine — we never generate. Every image is retouched by hand, frame by frame, and that includes frames a client generated themselves before sending them to us: we take those to catalogue standard rather than making new ones.
