Our process

How a timepiece image is finished.

Watches are the least forgiving product in the studio: a mirror-polished case reflects everything, and a sapphire crystal shows every light you own. Each image moves through the same four stages.

1 · Dial & crystal

Dust under the crystal, lint on the dial and hairline marks on indices are cleaned at full zoom. Crystal glare is reduced so printed text, sub-dials and the date window stay legible instead of washing out.

2 · Case & bracelet

Polished, brushed and two-tone surfaces are rebuilt where the lighting flattened them, keeping the line between finishes crisp. Bracelet links are evened out so the metal reads as one continuous piece.

3 · Reflection mapping

Softbox shapes, tripod legs and room reflections are removed or replaced with clean gradients. The aim is a case that looks lit, not a case that shows your studio back to the customer.

4 · Batch QC

A second retoucher checks the full set for matching dial colour, crown position, shadow weight and crop, so a model range looks like one coherent collection.

How long does watch retouching take?

Standard turnaround is 24 to 48 hours. Larger catalogues are scheduled in batches at an agreed daily volume, and rush delivery is available when a launch or marketplace deadline is fixed.

Can you remove reflections completely?

We remove distracting reflections such as softbox edges, tripods and room detail, while keeping the highlights that give a polished case its depth. Stripping every reflection makes metal look flat and fake, so we control them rather than delete them.

What file formats do you accept?

RAW, TIFF, PSD, PNG and JPEG. Send the highest quality original you have. We return layered PSD or flattened files in the size and colour profile your store or printer requires.

Are unreleased models kept confidential?

Yes. We sign NDAs on request, restrict access to the assigned retouchers, and delete or return files on an agreed retention schedule.
