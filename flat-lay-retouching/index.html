What the pass includes

Take out the styling. Keep the cloth.

StepWhat it means
Symmetry and alignmentSleeves, hems and plackets squared to the frame. Collars evened. The garment straight to the grid, not to the table it was shot on.
Wrinkles and fold linesPackaging creases, pin marks, tape and foam taken out — while the fabric's own texture and drape stay. More on wrinkle removal.
Shape correctionThe garment made to read as the cut it actually is, rather than the shape it happened to fall into on the table.
Cut-out and backgroundHand-drawn path to pure white or transparent, with the shadow rule the layout asks for applied the same way every time.
ColourMatched to the real fabric. When two frames of the same garment disagree, the spec sheet names which is the truth — agreed before the batch, not during it.

Sheer, lace, heavy texture and fine repeating pattern take longer than a cotton tee. Those are the frames worth sending in a test, not the easy ones.

FAQ

Flat lay retouching — questions

What is flat lay retouching?

The post-production pass on garments photographed laid flat from above. Symmetry and alignment, wrinkle and crease removal, shape correction, removal of pins, tape and foam, cut-out to pure white or transparent, and colour matched to the real fabric. Across a catalogue the decisive part is every garment sitting at the same size on the same baseline.

Flat lay or ghost mannequin — which should we shoot?

Flat lay is faster to shoot, cheaper to retouch, and suits pieces whose shape is obvious lying down. Ghost mannequin suits anything where fit is the selling point. Most catalogues need both; the mistake is shooting one way for everything.

How much does flat lay retouching cost?

It sits at the lower end of apparel post-production because there is no interior reconstruction, and falls further with monthly volume. Fabric moves it more than garment type — sheer, lace and fine pattern take longer than a cotton tee.

Can you remove wrinkles without flattening the fabric?

That is the craft. Styling creases, packaging folds and pin marks come out; texture and drape stay. Over-smoothed flat lay reads as plastic and is the commonest failure in cheap apparel retouching.

Do you use AI on flat lay images?

No. Hand-retouched frame by frame, never run through generative tools, never used as training data, and we sign your DPA saying so.

Can you match our existing flat lay style?

Yes. Send finished frames you are happy with alongside the test and we work to those, then write the spec sheet from what the test taught us and you confirm it.
