Said plainly

Steam it first. We mean that.

Post-production is cheaper than a reshoot. It is not cheaper than a steamer, and a well-prepared garment photographs better than a rescued one.

Retouching earns its place on what steaming cannot fix: fold lines set into the fabric, pins and clips holding a shape, creases that only show under studio light, and the frames you cannot reshoot because the sample has already gone back to the supplier. If a studio tells you post can replace garment prep, they are selling you hours rather than solving your problem.

Where it sits

Part of three different passes.

  • Flat lay — the most wrinkle-heavy work there is, because the garment has just come out of a polybag and been arranged by hand.
  • Ghost mannequin — creases around the pins and the neck joint, plus whatever the form pushes into the fabric.
  • On-model — the hardest, because the garment has to keep moving. Take out too much and the clothing looks painted on the model.

Usually quoted inside the service rather than as a line of its own. Rates published in full, and they fall as monthly volume rises.

Why cheap versions fail

The blur removes the crease and the cloth together.

A smoothing action takes out the wrinkle and the weave in one move. The result reads as a drawing of a garment rather than a photograph of one, and on a category page it is obvious the moment it sits beside a frame done properly.

It is a method problem rather than a skill problem, which is why it repeats on every frame once a supplier has chosen that method. Check it the same way every time: open the hardest fabric in the batch at 100% and look for the weave. If it is gone, the wrinkles were not removed — the fabric was. FAQ

Wrinkle removal — questions

How do you remove wrinkles from clothes in product photos?

By hand, frame by frame, separating two things that look alike. Packaging folds, styling creases, pin marks and tape shadows come out; weave, texture and drape stay. Done with dodge and burn and local tonal correction rather than blur, which is why it survives being zoomed into.

Why does cheap wrinkle removal look plastic?

Because a blur removes the crease and the weave together. The garment reads as a drawing rather than a photograph. It is a method problem, not a skill problem, so it repeats on every frame.

Should we steam the garment or fix it in post?

Steam first, always. Post is cheaper than a reshoot but not cheaper than a steamer. Retouching earns its place on fold lines set into the fabric, pins holding a shape, and frames you cannot reshoot because the sample has gone back.

Can you handle sheer and textured fabrics?

Yes, and those are the frames worth sending in a test. On satin a crease is a highlight, on knit it is a shadow between stitches, on a print the repeat has to stay aligned. Each needs a different approach.

Do you use AI to remove wrinkles?

No. Hand-retouched frame by frame, never run through generative tools, never used as training data, and we sign your DPA saying so.
