What comes back

Four numbers about your own catalogue.

Most arguments about retouching quality are arguments about taste and cannot be settled. These four can be, on a batch you have already paid for.

Frame dimensionThe pixel width and height of every frame you sent, listedTwo or three dimensions in one batch means the crop was decided per image rather than against a specification

The first two are arithmetic. The second two need eyes, which is the part you cannot script. All four are things you could check yourself — the method and the command are published on the catalogue consistency standard, so you can verify how we measure before you send anything.

Why this and not a sample

A free sample asks you to imagine a different supplier.

The usual offer in this industry is five free images, and we publish one too. It tests our standard, which is useful only once you have already decided to look for someone else.

An audit tests something more immediately useful: the thing you are paying for now. It uses your own files, returns numbers you can check against your own eyes, and answers a question you may not yet have put into words — why does the category page look patchy when every individual image looked fine on approval?

Three situations where buyers run this and find something:

  • The grid looks wrong and nobody can say why. Almost always background drift of two or three values, invisible on one frame and plain across forty.
  • Returns crept up and somebody suspects colour. The audit will not prove that on its own, but a set with no agreed colour reference frame is the first place to look.
  • Volume went up and quality went sideways. Dimensions splitting into two or three sizes part-way through a batch is the signature of work moved to unchecked hands at peak.

None of those people are shopping for a retoucher. All of them have a problem worth measuring.

Being straight about it

What the numbers will not tell you.

Measurement catches drift. It does not catch taste, and anyone who says otherwise is selling something.

A garment can be cut to exactly the right dimensions, on exactly the right white, with every frame identical — and still have a collar rebuilt in the wrong shape. The four checks tell you whether a supplier works to a standard. They do not tell you whether that standard is any good. Where the measurements come back clean, the reply will say so plainly, and the honest conclusion is that there is no supplier problem to solve.

We also measure ourselves on the same four, and publish the result. Twenty-four finished garments across seven material problems — tailoring, ribbed knitwear, sheer silk, denim, moulded activewear, waxed cotton and lace. Every frame 2600 × 3400. Background brightness 255 on all of them. No variance. Those numbers are on the standard page with the method, so you can hold us to the same test you are asking us to run.

FAQ

Retouching audit — questions

Why would you measure a competitor's work for free?

Because the measurement is the argument. A studio that cannot hold one dimension and one background value across a set has a process problem, and the numbers say so without anyone needing to be persuaded of anything. If your current supplier measures clean, the honest answer is that you do not need to change supplier — and that is what you will be told.

Can I just run it myself?

Yes. The method and the command are published on the catalogue consistency standard. The first two checks take about twenty minutes on two hundred frames with any tool that reports pixel dimensions and pixel values. The audit exists because most people would rather not spend the twenty minutes.

Which images should I send?

The difficult ones, not the clean ones. Drift shows across a range, so a spread of several products from one category tells you more than twenty frames of a single item. If there is a hard material in the batch — lace, sheer fabric, fur, wire, anything translucent — include it.

Do I have to tell you who does my retouching now?

No. It is useful context and the field is optional. The measurement does not depend on it, and no supplier is ever named by us.

Will the audit tell me whether the retouching is good?

Only partly, and this is worth being clear about. Measurement catches drift, not taste. A set can be perfectly consistent and still be poorly judged. Where that is what we see, the reply will say so rather than hiding behind the numbers.

What if I am not looking to change supplier?

That is the most common case and it is fine. Plenty of buyers run this to settle an internal argument, or to have something concrete to take back to the supplier they already use. There is no follow-up sequence attached to it.

Do you use generative AI?

No. We correct and refine — we never generate. Every image is retouched by hand, frame by frame, and that includes frames a client generated themselves before sending them to us. Your files never pass through a generative tool, and they are never used as training data for any model.
