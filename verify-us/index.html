How to check us out

You are about to send product files to a studio you have not met, in a country you are not in, with no client names on its website. That is a reasonable thing to be careful about. This page is what we would want to read if we were you.

Start with what counts against us

It is quicker if we say it first.

  • The company is new. Retouch Atelier was established in 2026. The people have more than eleven years in commercial retouching between them, but the company does not, and we never imply it does
  • There are no client names or logos anywhere on this site. Not one
  • There are no published reviews yet. There is a Clutch profile and it carries none at the time of writing
  • Production is in Bangladesh, which is a real consideration for some buyers and we would rather you weigh it now than later

If any of those is disqualifying, it is disqualifying today rather than after a test batch, and that is better for both of us. If they are not, the rest of this page is how to check the things that can be checked.

1. Why there are no client names, and why that will not change

The work is white-label by policy. Our name does not appear on a client's delivery; their name does not appear on our site. Those are the same rule read from two ends.

It is worth being direct about what that means for you. The rule that stops us naming them is the rule that stops the next studio naming you. A supplier who shows you a wall of client logos is telling you exactly what they would do with yours.

So the absence is a constraint we chose, and it costs us. It is also the only part of our proof we cannot hand over, which is why everything else on this page is designed to be checkable without it.

3. The one test that settles it

Everything above is paper. The work is not.

Send five frames from a real job — ideally the four that break suppliers: something translucent, something pale on a pale background, something with fine edges, and one where a real contact shadow has to survive. They come back free, within six hours, with no commitment and no payment detail taken.

Judge at 100 per cent rather than at thumbnail size, and look across the set rather than at the best frame. If it is not good, you have lost an afternoon and learned something about us at no cost. If you want to run the same test against a supplier you already use, the retouching audit measures finished frames you have already paid for.

4. What we sign before a single file moves

  • An NDA as standard, before files are sent rather than after
  • Your DPA, not ours. Standard Contractual Clauses or the UK Addendum where the transfer requires them
  • A written undertaking that client images are never used to train any model
  • Deletion on an agreed date, written down rather than assumed

The detail your legal team will ask for is set out on the security and confidentiality page, including how files move and who can reach them.

5. Where the work is actually done

In Bangladesh. We state it on the site rather than behind a Western front address, because you will find out anyway and a supplier who hides it has told you something about how they handle other questions.

58 retouchers work across three shifts, six days a week, closed Sunday, which is how a London or New York working day always overlaps a live shift rather than a mailbox. Sustained capacity is 1,800+ images a day and up to 40,000 a month.

For an emergency or an unusually large drop, some retouching may be done by contracted partner studios. We would rather write that down than let you discover it. The part that does not move: every image passes our own in-house QC before it reaches you, and the partner studios work under written confidentiality terms equivalent to the ones we signed with you.

6. What to ask any offshore supplier, including us

These are the questions that separate suppliers. Ask them of us and of whoever else you are considering, and compare the answers rather than the brochures.

  • Who physically touches the files, and where do the files sit while they do it?
  • Will you sign my DPA, or only offer your own terms?
  • Can my images be used to train anything? Will you put that in writing?
  • What happens to the files afterwards, and on what date?
  • Are the people who did my test the people who will do the work?
  • What is the price in writing before the first batch, not after it?
  • What happens when volume triples for a month — who does it, and does the standard hold?

A supplier who answers all seven without hedging is worth a test. One who answers five is worth a smaller one.

7. What we have not earned yet

We would rather name this than let it sit unsaid.

We do not have reviews. We do not have years. We do not have logos we are allowed to show. Those are earned over time and we are early.

What we do have is a rate card you can read without a call, a measurement anyone can reproduce, a written position on AI that can go in a contract, a stated location, and a free test on your own files that costs you nothing but the sending. For a first batch, that is the honest set, and we would rather you judge it than a sentence about passion.

FAQ

Common questions

Is Retouch Atelier a new company?

Yes. Retouch Atelier was established in 2026. The team behind it has more than eleven years in commercial retouching, but the company itself is new and we do not imply otherwise. If a long corporate track record is what you need, we are not the right supplier and you should say so early.

Why are there no client names or logos on the site?

Because the work is white-label by policy. Our name does not appear on a client's delivery, and their name does not appear on our site. The same rule that stops us naming them is the rule that stops the next studio naming you. It is a deliberate constraint, not an absence of work.

Does Retouch Atelier have published reviews?

Not on any independent platform yet. There is a Clutch profile and it carries no reviews at the time of writing. A small number of client comments appear on our own pages without the company name; nobody outside the studio has checked them, so read them as our word rather than as evidence. We would rather say that plainly than have you discover it. The way to judge the work before anyone has reviewed it is the free test on your own frames.

How can I verify Retouch Atelier's quality claims without trusting the studio?

Two ways. First, the consistency standard page publishes the measured figures for a finished set — 24 garments, every frame 2600 by 3400 pixels, background brightness 255 with no variance — together with the ImageMagick command to reproduce the measurement on any batch, including ours. Second, send five of your own frames: they come back free within six hours and you judge the result rather than the claim.

Where is the work actually done?

In Bangladesh, stated openly on the site rather than obscured behind a Western front address. For an emergency or an unusually large drop some retouching may be done by contracted partner studios under written confidentiality terms, and every image still passes our own in-house QC before it is delivered.

What will Retouch Atelier sign before any files are sent?

An NDA as standard, and the client's own DPA. Standard Contractual Clauses or the UK Addendum where the transfer requires them. Client images are never used to train any model, and files are deleted on the agreed date.

What questions should I ask any offshore retouching supplier?

Who physically touches the files and where they sit; whether the supplier will sign your DPA rather than offer their own terms; whether your images can be used for training; what happens to the files afterwards and when; whether the people who did your test are the people who will do the work; and what the price is in writing before the first batch rather than after it.
