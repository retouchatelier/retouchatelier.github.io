Why it is paid

A free sample tests the retouching. It does not test the fit.

Five free images show you our standard. They do not show you whether we can hold that standard across a batch, inside your pipeline, against your clock — which is the only thing that matters once volume starts.

The fit test is real production on your live files, so it is charged as production: the published per-image rate for the category, with nothing added. You keep the finished frames whatever you decide afterwards.

What we need

Send the difficult frames, not the easy ones.

  • Fifty to a hundred frames from a live job — a real batch with a real deadline, not a curated set
  • Your specification or style guide — however rough, even a few lines in an email
  • Your naming convention and the delivery format your own system expects
  • The deadline the batch is actually due — we work to that, not to a comfortable one
What you get back

Four things, not just the files.

  • The finished frames to your specification, in your naming convention
  • A QC report — what held across the batch, what varied, and where
  • A note on what your specification leaves ambiguous — the part that costs money later, written down before it does
  • A rate and a daily throughput figure for your actual volume, not a range
What it costs

Who this is for

Five hundred images a month, usually daily.

FAQ

Production fit test — questions

What is a production fit test?

A paid pilot of fifty to a hundred frames taken from a live job rather than a sample set. We work to your written specification, your naming convention and the deadline the batch is actually due, then return the finished frames with a QC report. It tests whether the studio fits your production, not just whether it can retouch an image.

Why is it paid when you also offer a free test edit?

They answer different questions. The free test edit of five images shows you the standard of the retouching. The fit test shows you whether we can hold that standard across a batch, inside your pipeline, against your clock. That is real production work on your live files, so it is charged as production work.

What does it cost?

The published per-image rate for the category, with nothing added for the pilot. Fifty to a hundred frames at your band. If you go ahead, the pilot fee comes off your first invoice.

What do you need from us?

Fifty to a hundred frames from a live job, your specification or style guide however rough it is, your naming convention and delivery format, and the real deadline. Send the difficult frames rather than the easy ones.

What do we get back?

The finished frames to your specification and naming, a QC report covering what held and what varied across the batch, a written note on anything your specification leaves ambiguous, and a rate and daily throughput figure for your actual volume.

Is there a minimum volume?

The fit test is built for catalogues running five hundred images a month or more, usually daily. Below that a high-volume studio is the wrong choice and we will say so rather than take the work.
