How to read any of it

Four things move a per-image price, and only one is on the page.

  • Turnaround tier. The clearest finding in this index: Path publishes five turnaround options — 6, 12, 24, 48 and 96 hours — and the price moves with the clock. A quoted "from" price is the slow-lane price. Our own 24–48 hours is flat and carries no rush surcharge, which makes us dearer at the bottom of the band and cheaper in a hurry.
  • Complexity. Every provider in the first table prices per image after looking at it. A solid cotton t-shirt and a lace bodysuit are not the same job and no honest rate card pretends otherwise.
  • Order size. Most providers move to unpublished custom rates above roughly a thousand images a month, so for a catalogue buyer none of the published numbers is the number you will pay. Treat this table as a way to shortlist, not as a quote.
  • What the price includes. QC layers, revision policy, whether a written specification exists, who checks a peak batch. These never appear on a rate card and they are what decides the real cost.

The arithmetic worth doing before optimising a rate: two cents an image across five thousand images a month is a hundred dollars. One rejected batch that has to be reworked, or one category page that ships looking like patchwork, costs more than that. The cheapest supplier and the lowest total cost are frequently different suppliers.

Method

How this index is built, so you can argue with it.

  • Primary evidence only in the first table. A figure appears there if we opened the provider's own pricing page and read it. Everything else goes in the second table, labelled.
  • Every figure carries a date. Prices change; an undated price table is worthless within a quarter.
  • Sorted by price, not by preference. We are in it, and we are above the market leader on comparable services. That is the honest position and hiding it would make the rest of the page untrustworthy.
  • Empty beats guessed. Where an exclusions cell could not be filled honestly for another provider, it stays as what we could confirm rather than an inference about their business.
  • Corrections are welcome and credited. Including from the providers listed.

FAQ

Retouching prices — questions

How much does product photo retouching cost per image in 2026?

Read off providers' own pricing pages on 2 November 2026, published starting rates run from about $0.25 for a drop shadow to about $3.99 for vector conversion. Clipping path starts around $0.39, background removal and ghost mannequin around $0.89, and general product retouching around $0.69 to $0.95. These are starting prices for small orders at standard turnaround, and most providers quote custom rates above roughly a thousand images a month.

How much does ghost mannequin retouching cost?

Path lists it from $0.89 per image and we list it from $1.00, both read on 2 November 2026. Third parties report Hello Edits at $0.49 to $0.69 and Clipping Path Zone from $0.49, neither confirmed on their own pricing pages. Garments with interior structure cost more than the starting rate everywhere.

Why do published retouching prices vary so much?

Because the headline number is rarely comparable. Turnaround tier, image complexity, order size and what the price includes all move it, and only the last one tells you anything about quality.

Is a cheaper per-image rate actually cheaper?

Not reliably. The per-image rate does not price rework, the time spent writing a specification that should already exist, or a category page that has to be redone because the set drifted. That is why this index carries an exclusions column rather than just a number.

Are you in your own index?

Yes, and not at the top of it. On comparable services we are above both Path and Pixelz on published rates. We would rather publish that than publish a comparison in which we happen to win.

Why publish this at all?

Because every other comparison in this category is written by a provider that ranks itself first, and none of them dates or links its figures. A buyer deserves one table they can check. If it costs us a few price-led enquiries, those were not the enquiries we are built for.
